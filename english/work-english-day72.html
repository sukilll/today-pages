Work English Day 72

Meetings & Calls
Word of the Day
blockers /ˈblɒkərz/
阻碍因素（复数）
Things that are preventing progress on a task or project. In meetings, asking "what are the blockers?" means you want to know what's stopping the team from moving forward.
Before we wrap up, let's go through the blockers — I want to make sure no one's stuck.
The main blocker right now is the API spec hasn't been finalized yet.
Sentence Patterns
"What are the blockers?"
站会 · 项目同步
有什么阻碍？卡在哪了？
Before we close, what are the blockers? Anyone need help?
Let's do a quick round — what are the blockers for this sprint?
"Can we take that offline?"
会议中 · 话题偏离时
这个会后单独聊？（避免占用所有人时间）
That's a good point — can we take that offline? It's a bit out of scope for this call.
I'd love to dig into that — can we take it offline after the meeting?
"I want to make sure we're aligned on ___."
决策确认 · 会议结束前
我想确认大家对……达成一致
Before we end, I want to make sure we're aligned on the rollout timeline.
I want to make sure we're aligned on priorities before anyone starts building.
Mini Scenario
It's the end of a product sync. You're wrapping up and want to make sure nothing is stuck.

"Okay, we're almost out of time. Any blockers before we close? Tianchun, you mentioned something about the feed rendering — can we take that offline? I want to dig in but don't want to hold everyone. And just to close the loop — I want to make sure we're aligned on the deploy window. We said Thursday EOD, right?"
Quick Tip
💡
"Take that offline" sounds polite, not dismissive — it signals you value the topic, just not in front of everyone. In Chinese meetings, people sometimes let discussions run long to avoid seeming rude. In English-speaking teams, redirecting a tangent with "can we take this offline?" is considered considerate, not a shutdown.
